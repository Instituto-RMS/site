# Motion DNA

Motion expresses state, continuity, or atmosphere. Always provide a reduced-motion path.

## `functional-feedback`

Use immediate state transitions for focus, selection, progress, save, failure, and completion. Duration follows comprehension, not spectacle. Best default for workspaces, dashboards, forms, and conversations.

## `weighted-spring`

Use settling or overshoot when an object should feel physical: panels, cards, draggable media, or staged controls. Keep destructive and precision actions controlled. Never apply one spring curve to every element.

## `mechanical-step`

Use discrete reveals, frame changes, scans, or deliberate ticks for technical, archival, or raw interfaces. Preserve readable intermediate states. Avoid stutter on text entry, navigation, and critical forms.

## `cinematic-scroll`

Use scroll to sequence scenes, reveal framed media, shift crops, or transition between narrative chapters. The contact-sheet progression must remain understandable without animation. Avoid scroll hijacking and long pinned sections without escape.

## Motion budget

Select one motion language. Additional motion stays functional. No required loops, reveals, parallax, or hover transforms.
